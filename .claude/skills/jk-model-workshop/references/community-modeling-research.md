# Community methods for character models, animation and player integration

Choose the route by the change the character needs. The Massassi modelers documented several workable approaches; they also left useful reports of what failed. This guide translates those contributions into present authoring choices for JKDF2 and MotS character, creature, player and weapon assets.

| Desired result | Useful route | Main tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New face, costume or faction quickly | Reskin a permitted existing model | Keeps the original silhouette and motion |
| Original humanoid or a stylized alien playable sooner | Fit new parts around an established numbered rig | Body proportions must accommodate the reused motion |
| Familiar movements with significantly different proportions | Adapt the existing KEY set to the altered joints | Every reachable action and transition needs checking |
| Different gait, anatomy or deliberate character performance | Custom hierarchy, KEYs and PUP mappings | Adds animation, attachment, sound and gameplay-event work |
| Matching first-person hands | Separate native POV models and keys, initially for a small weapon set | A selectable third-person skin does not supply these assets |
| A native POV arrangement that cannot render cleanly | Consider a scripted world-model workaround | Adds camera, clipping, lifecycle and multiplayer work; test before committing |

For TDIR and JKE authorship, asset comparisons and texture tiers, use the [JKE/TDIR/Shred research](jke-tdir-shred-research.md). For the native allocation and export contract, use [compatibility](compatibility.md). These community techniques complement those references.

## Stock motion, adapted motion, or new motion

**A stock-motion version is a legitimate design option.** Mike “LikWid” Watson's *Making Player 3dos* builds a Road Runner around existing body slots, including parts whose visual function differs from the original anatomy. His method also keeps material numbering consistent while assembling meshes. The portable lesson is to plan what each numbered part does, fit geometry around its motion, and remap materials explicitly. Tiny hidden construction meshes were part of that historical tool workflow, not a requirement for a modern exporter. [LikWid tutorial, catalogued 28 March 1999](https://www.massassi.net/tutorials/player3dos/player3dos.html)

In *Character Models and Animations*, F-Body advised matching Kyle's joint positions and the surrounding geometry when reusing Kyle's keys. Hell Raiser offered a middle route: use the same nodes at changed positions and adapt the keys with KeyEd. This supports offering a stock fit, an adapted animation set, and a fully custom set separately. KeyEd is a historical tool lead here; its current executable and output have not been validated by this research. [F-Body, 4 May; Hell Raiser, 5–6 May 2006](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_41104_page_1.html)

For a new alien, make one stock-motion prototype before deciding that all animations need replacement. Test the most revealing actions early: raised weapon, crouch, run-and-fire, jump, saber motion if applicable, death and respawn. If the shoulders, reach or feet cannot work without major distortion, budget an adapted or custom animation set. This is our workflow recommendation, not a claim that a forum author tested that complete sequence.

## Build the hierarchy deliberately

**Shred18 supplied a complete character hierarchy procedure**, not merely a general recommendation to use JED. In response to HAcKSaw's new SP/MP character, he described assigning parents through the body, placing the pivots visually, exporting the selected meshes and correcting insertion height. The enduring method is to establish the parent chain first, place anatomical pivots independently of mesh shape, and check the character's placement after export. His hip-root example suits that character design; it is not a universal rule for every creature. [Shred18, 4 June 2005, post 2](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_32931_page_1.html)

BoBo_Fett later identified JED's **Tools → 3DO hierarchy → Edit Mesh X,Y,Z** operation for visible pivot adjustment. For a modern authoring tool, expose the equivalent native pivot and local-transform values instead of guessing from an object's displayed center. [BoBo_Fett, 26 August 2015, post 4](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_67602_page_1.html)

Insertion height is a separate issue from articulation. Nikumubeki suggested a specific `INSERT OFFSET` to Darth J, who confirmed the correction worked. Use the chosen base character and the intended ground contact as your reference; do not apply that one numerical offset to characters of every size. [Nikumubeki and Darth J, 29 April 2012](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_60257_page_1.html)

Invisible nodes can still have visible consequences. Ping_Me's animation tutorial calls attention to the otherwise inconspicuous right-hand attachment point and uses a weapon as an orientation reference. Preserve attachment semantics even when a part has no visible body mesh. Its historical visible-part count should not be copied as a universal hierarchy or KEY allocation count. [Ping_Me, *Animation Made Easy!!!!*, catalogued 14 June 1999](https://www.massassi.net/tutorials/animation/)

When an editor reports a circular hierarchy, inspect parent indices and cycles. In eggtooth's thread, darthslaw considered self-parenting and later cited a JED version-specific hierarchy-display fix. The discussion also contains advice to ignore the warning, but it does not establish that arbitrary hierarchy warnings are safe. An editor's supported node count is a different limit from an animated engine's storage. [darthslaw and eggtooth, 29 September–2 October 2005](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_36140_page_1.html)

## Graft and simplify parts without losing the native contract

Ruthven suggested splitting a complicated replacement head into child groups beneath the head node. darthslaw separately suspected duplicated texture vertices when a model failed after editing. These are useful design and diagnostic ideas, but that thread does not finish with a demonstrated repair. For a detailed head or armor piece, preserve the existing numbered motion nodes, plan additional rigid children using the [KEY reservation and allocation rules](compatibility.md#preserve-the-numbered-animation-contract), and count positions, texture vertices and faces independently. Do not adopt its historical 256-face statement as the current verified parser limit. [Ruthven, SavageX378, darthslaw and rob_whatman, 13–16 February 2006](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_39383_page_1.html)

A broader limits discussion contains a corrected mesh-count claim and a working-model counterexample from ProdigyOddigy. It is valuable precisely because the numbers were contested. Use measured native limits and a complete animation-domain check, not multiplication of remembered mesh and face maxima into a promised total model budget. [JediKirby, ProdigyOddigy and Z-man12, 8–10 July 2005](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_33977_page_1.html)

**Deletion order and topology can explain an MP selection crash.** F-Body reported repairing one model by deleting faces before their vertices in his JOKE workflow; deleting vertices first had left degenerate faces. Ruthven and darthslaw also discussed re-exporting and numeric notation. The thread's final success does not isolate every proposed cause. Check the emitted indices and degenerate faces; do not zero small scientific-notation coordinates as a generic repair. An editor preview is only one consumer of the file. [F-Body, Ruthven and darthslaw, 30 August–1 September 2005](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_35412_page_1.html)

For weapon grafts, Quib Mask described replacing a mesh block while adjusting its material references. The useful choice is between replacing one prepared part and rebuilding the whole hierarchy. Prefer the smaller change when its joints already work, but parse and remap the material table rather than assuming copied face numbers still mean the same textures. This thread does not establish a completed repair of BigCarm's model. [Quib Mask and BigCarm, 9 August 2005](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_34863_page_1.html)

## Author motions and events together

Start a custom animation workflow with a small, readable gesture. PJB's tutorial builds a wave through shoulder, forearm and hand, adds delayed hand movement, then returns the body to its starting pose. Use that as an economical round-trip exercise before making an entire gait: author, export, load, play, and inspect the return or loop boundary. [PJB, *Keys*, catalogued 2 March 2003](https://www.massassi.net/tutorials/keys/)

**Blended actions need node types as well as track priorities.** ProdigyOddigy reported that a firing animation incorrectly overrode the running legs until the hierarchy's per-node settings were corrected. The pinned source narrows the terminology: `rdPuppet_BuildJointMatrices` selects each track's high or low priority using the bitwise intersection of **KEY type and hierarchy node type**. Preserve those fields and test simultaneous actions; changing a generic flags field or a single priority is not a complete recipe. [ProdigyOddigy, 11 August 2005](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_34905_page_1.html); [OpenJKDF2, rdPuppet.c](https://github.com/shinyquagsire23/OpenJKDF2/blob/0dbd1ea8bb2211c158f6af928b5e4fb14dfa0dac/src/Engine/rdPuppet.c#L233)

Ruthven connected silent custom character animations with missing event markers; darthslaw explained placing them at particular frames in Puppet Jedi. Retain and retime the appropriate contact and action cues when replacing a clip. [Ruthven and darthslaw, 1 September 2005](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_35474_page_1.html)

Markers are not solely cosmetic. The inspected source dispatches marker callbacks, and the default callback includes footsteps, AI weapon firing and saber-damage enable/disable behavior. An attack that looks correct can therefore remain mechanically wrong. rob_whatman's report separately describes fixing mismatched animation nodes and then still lacking attack damage; Stormtrooper suggested comparing a stock attack marker. Compare the matching actor/weapon route, not an arbitrary attack's marker numbers. [rob_whatman and Stormtrooper, 7–9 September 2004](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_24352_page_1.html); [OpenJKDF2, sithPuppet.c](https://github.com/shinyquagsire23/OpenJKDF2/blob/0dbd1ea8bb2211c158f6af928b5e4fb14dfa0dac/src/Engine/sithPuppet.c)

For template-based actor integration, check that a puppet is initialized before debugging a silent `PlayKey`. Evil_Giraffe reported that adding a puppet reference allowed a test key to run; Edward explained puppet roles and rob_whatman distinguished direct KEY playback from PUP action mappings. The source also requires a render puppet for `PlayKey`. The original thread involves a ship; only this model-animation lesson is retained here. [30 September 2004 discussion](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_25381_page_1.html); [OpenJKDF2, PlayKey](https://github.com/shinyquagsire23/OpenJKDF2/blob/0dbd1ea8bb2211c158f6af928b5e4fb14dfa0dac/src/Cog/sithCogFunctionThing.c#L989)

## Select skins and scope first-person work separately

CosmoDNA's skin tutorial joins texture editing to actual player selection: MAT references, palette preparation and a `models.dat` entry all matter. Its registry-count example is specific to its base file. Derive changes from the target installation, use a mod overlay and consistent resource names, and install the required package on each participating client. Palette behavior and transparency need their own renderer checks; do not convert old paint-program restrictions into present universal rules. [CosmoDNA, *Skins*, catalogued 23 July 1998](https://www.massassi.net/tutorials/skins/)

For SP, zagibu proposed either replacing the expected model resource or changing the selection through COG. Xzero Phoenix and ZeqMacaw then helped distinguish an archive-tool failure from the skin change; KnightRider2000 reported success after the corrected package. These are integration choices, not evidence that arbitrary combinations of mods are compatible. [30 June–1 July 2005 discussion](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_33731_page_1.html)

A third-person hand attachment, a first-person weapon-and-hands model, and a screenshot with no hands are separate deliverables. Xzero's suggestion in *Empty Hands* was acknowledged by SiMoN as useful for the requested view. It does not establish custom POV assets or remote-client appearance. The source independently exposes separate POV model and animation operations. [Xzero and SiMoN, 13 April 2009](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_53913_page_1.html); [OpenJKDF2, POV model/key operations](https://github.com/shinyquagsire23/OpenJKDF2/blob/0dbd1ea8bb2211c158f6af928b5e4fb14dfa0dac/src/Cog/jkCog.c#L246)

Offer native POV hands for one or two priority weapons first. In *POV-Modeling*, F-Body describes drawbacks of the scripted approach and ZeqMacaw points back to constructing native internal models. The linked tutorial and workaround were not executed here. [F-Body and ZeqMacaw, 7 June 2007](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_47271_page_1.html)

The longer *3do first person problems* thread gives both routes and their costs: darthslaw supplies a Seifer-style workaround, Z-man12 reports success, ProdigyOddigy discusses reset crashes, and JM explains native depth/draw-order behavior. zagibu explicitly disputes the suggestion that one mesh necessarily solves it. Test native ordering first; treat a scripted visible thing as a separate implementation with respawn, camera and wall-clipping tests. [22–23 August 2005 discussion](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_35188_page_1.html)

Renderer differences matter here. The pinned port has distinct native-style no-depth-read/write and SDL/retro depth-buffer paths for POV drawing. That supports testing the actual target renderer rather than assuming a source-port screenshot proves original-engine POV ordering. [OpenJKDF2, jkPlayer_DrawPov](https://github.com/shinyquagsire23/OpenJKDF2/blob/0dbd1ea8bb2211c158f6af928b5e4fb14dfa0dac/src/World/jkPlayer.c#L839)

## Diagnose lighting and LOD with controlled comparisons

In *Fully Lit 3do*, Ruthven recommends inspecting extra light, F-Body identifies the face field, and darthslaw raises duplicate UV entries as an untested explanation for export failures. The requester later suspects other scene state; the thread does not isolate one final cause. For a character, compare the parsed mesh/face lighting modes, extra-light values, normals and UV counts with the previous working export. Change one cause at a time. Do not blindly replace text or conclude that every JOKE version always produces the same defect. [Ruthven, F-Body, darthslaw and jedi I, 13–17 June 2006](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_41914_page_1.html)

Two LOD discussions remain unresolved even after participants check settings or declared geosets. Their value is diagnostic: the presence of several geosets is not evidence that transitions work at useful distances. For a player or creature, walk the camera outward and back, inspect silhouette and attachment continuity, and record the exact executable and renderer. The original static-object settings are not a character recipe. [eggtooth and Axis, October 2005](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_36447_page_1.html); [jedi I and Stormtrooper, 18 July 2006](https://forums.massassi.net/Editing_Forums/Jedi_Knight_and_Mysteries_of_the_Sith_Editing_Forum/thread_42634_page_1.html)

## Practical starting plans

- **Playable alien soon:** use permitted stock-motion joints, original silhouette and textures, and a small number of planned rigid details. Check attachment orientation and ground height first. Defer matching POV hands until the third-person character passes selection, motion and connected-client checks.
- **Expressive custom creature:** preserve or design the numbered hierarchy, make a short gesture and one attack, validate blending and event cues, then expand the required action set and PUP mappings. Test the intended SP actor behavior before producing a full animation library.
- **Matching hands as an optional upgrade:** support a named weapon subset with native POV geometry and keys. Check mount, fire, dismount, near-wall behavior and respawn. Offer the scripted workaround only if its added implementation cost solves a demonstrated native-rendering problem.

These are our synthesized plans. The historical authors receive credit for the techniques and observations above, not for testing our package or endorsing its conclusions.

## Sources and limits

The [source ledger](evidence/community-modeling-sources.json) records 20 selected forum threads, four tutorials, their authors and dates, retrieval receipts, corroborating code and excluded leads. Nineteen threads are new to the earlier JKE/TDIR reference; the limits discussion is revisited. Texts were read in full where marked; tutorial images, forum attachments and legacy executables were not inspected or run for this reference. Source-port code is corroboration of a mechanism in that revision, not blanket certification of an original executable.
